NARA Presidential Records roster

Teasoups is an American politician who served as Speaker of the House and President of the United States. She won the Summer and Fall 2024 presidential elections and headed an administration that continued until March 2025. Her executive program addressed Cabinet reporting, public records, agency policy, judicial selection, employment, funding and federal organization. NARA’s principal presidential roster numbers her as the 53rd President, while a separate second-term entry uses the number 54.[2]

Election and first recorded administration

Teasoups’s accession is commonly dated to 22 July 2024, while her first presidential inauguration took place on 28 July. The exact formal accession date is uncertain.[3]

Teasoups campaigned against incumbent President GeorgeGodsent in the Summer 2024 election. A presidential debate was advertised for 29 June. The Federal Election Commission certified her victory with 388 votes. Her first inauguration was held on 28 July.[4]

Four Federal Register orders signed by Teasoups are dated July 28, 2024. Executive Order 001 established a Federal Register under the White House Office of Policy and Administration for agency policies, weekly reports, presidential orders, and other designated material. It also directed review of pre-existing executive orders, which the President could repeal or replace.[5]

Executive Order 002 required Cabinet secretaries and agency directors to send weekly reports to the White House Chief of Staff, due Saturdays at 5 p.m. Eastern time beginning July 28. It specified reporting subjects, excluded classified material, and provided for archiving and potential public release. Executive Order 003 required Cabinet departments and agencies to submit policies describing their hiring, internal operations, structure, and goals for White House review and archival publication.[6]

Executive Order 004 created a five-member Judicial Selection Committee appointed by the President. The committee was to review applicants and present two candidates for each judicial vacancy to the President, who would select a nominee for Senate confirmation. Its text prescribed committee terms, procedures, and a code of conduct.[7]

Further executive orders

Executive Order 005, dated August 12, 2024, capped an individual's executive-branch employment at three jobs, with stated exceptions including military service and certain White House staff positions. It required agencies to audit staff and allowed a 48-hour period to comply before enforcement.[8]

Executive Order 006, dated August 26, established a Federal Exchange under the Treasury Department to coordinate agency uniform funding. It specified eligibility and request procedures, including a minimum allocation of ten funds per eligible agent. Executive Order 007, dated October 2, transferred the Department of Veterans Affairs under the Department of Defense as a subdepartment and provided for a presidentially appointed Secretary of Veterans Affairs who would report to the Secretary of Defense.[9]

Second-term record and ordinal discrepancy

Teasoups began a second administration with Vice President Rathuzen in November 2024. Executive Order 008, dated 23 November, reorganized the Department of Homeland Security, established an Office of Homeland Security Investigations and reassigned the Federal Protective Service’s mission. NARA catalogued the second administration separately under the number 54.[10]

Executive Order 009, dated December 26, 2024, established the Office of Management and Budget within the Executive Office of the President and transferred the Treasury Department's functions, personnel, assets, and authorities to it. The order provided that existing Treasury regulations would continue until changed by the new office.[11]

NARA's captured roster also contains a separate card titled “Rathuzen, 54th,” although Rathuzen's inauguration as President was announced in March 2025. The Teasoups and Rathuzen cards therefore both use the 54th ordinal. The cards, order numbering, and announcements do not explain whether this reflects a roster error, a repeated numbering convention, or distinct counts, and this article preserves the labels as recorded rather than choosing between them.[12]

End of recorded presidential service

Teasoups and Rathuzen were scheduled to take their oaths on 23 November 2024, and her Executive Order 008 described that date as the start of the second term. Rathuzen subsequently became President at the inauguration of 23 March 2025.[13]


2024 FEC election records

The Federal Election Commission certified Teasoups as the winner of the Summer 2024 presidential election with 388 votes. Its Fall election certificate named Teasoups and Rathuzen as the leading ticket and scheduled their inauguration for 23 November. Election announcements also used the display name blanket for the winning presidential candidate.[14]

The party system remained active in 2026. The National Elections Administrator reported separate presidential primary winners for Forward and Pioneer in March. A later owner-issued update said the Spring presidential election’s initial count was under review and announced corrected final totals of Pioneer candidate Bathamza, 117, and Forward candidate warlockings, 109. The owner’s account described alleged access to live vote data and bias in filtering as the reason for the review; that explanation is attributed to the notice. In June, the FEC said the Board of Owners and interim leadership had approved official governing platforms for Forward and Pioneer. These are dated 2026 developments and do not establish uninterrupted official status between the 2024 notices and the 2026 reforms.[15]

House speakership

Before her presidency, Teasoups served as Speaker in July 2024. Two certifications issued on 8 July announced her election over Jjc0308: the earlier reported eleven votes to nine, while the later recorded thirteen to nine. The later notice explicitly certified a majority of the sitting House.[16][17]

Anthonytheking9291 succeeded her later that month and was sworn in on 22 July. Her transition to the presidency followed shortly afterward.[18][19]
