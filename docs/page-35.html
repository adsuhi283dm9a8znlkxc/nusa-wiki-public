United States Congress (nUSA)

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

Legislative institution in the municipal government The United States Congress is the bicameral federal legislature, comprising the Senate and House of Representatives. Its work includes legislation, nominations, treaties, impeachment proceedings and constitutional amendments.[1]

Organization and work

Congressional business proceeds through the separate chambers and their committees. Measures can await committee consideration, floor votes or presidential action; proposals, active laws, repealed measures and failed items have different legal and procedural statuses. The presence of a proposal in congressional business does not make it law.[2]

The chambers exercise distinct responsibilities. In November 2017, the House rejected a proposed declaration of war following a North Korean threat against the Capitol.[3][4]

Institutional changes

On 15 July 2023, the Great Reset voided existing federal laws, case precedent, congressional resolutions, motions and awards. Colorado criminal law and the United States Code were designated to apply during the interim.[5]

On 17 July, Memorandum 001 established archival requirements, legislative regulations and restrictions on legislation alongside a new Constitution. New NARA and Congress records systems accompanied the authorization to resume normal business under the directives.[6]

The ninety-eighth Congress began on 19 August 2024. NARA required bills to carry correct session labels for archival inclusion. The constitutional framework has changed through successive versions, discussed in Constitutions of nUSA, 2024 Constitution of nUSA and 2025 constitutional readoption in nUSA.[7]

Records

Congress maintains records of legislative business separately from each chamber's leadership, membership, committees and rules. NARA's preservation requirements make recordkeeping part of parliamentary administration, supporting the continuity of the legislature's institutional history.[8]

Constitutional conflict and reconstruction

Congress's dealings with the judiciary in 2019 revealed the limits of its institutional power. The Supreme Court struck the principal provisions creating the State of Columbia because the legislatures of Washington and Las Vegas had not consented to their union. It later invalidated three of the four articles used to remove President Procursive, while leaving his removal intact. In July it also struck S. 147, which had assigned attorney licensing to executive administration.[9][10][11]

During the 2022 reconstruction, Congress became a forum for reconsidering both the governing framework and the practical operation of public service. On 25 April it entered a constitutional convention after adopting concurrent resolutions and obtaining Supreme Court consent. In July it passed the Domestic Protection Act, distinguishing foreign citizenship from service in a foreign civil office.[12]

The Federal Election Commission's interim schedule renewed both chambers through temporary arrangements of sixteen representatives and ten senators. In November the House adopted new standing rules covering leadership, committees, conduct and legislative procedure. Together, the electoral and procedural reforms sought to restore the legislature's capacity to conduct business as well as the occupancy of its seats.[13][14]
