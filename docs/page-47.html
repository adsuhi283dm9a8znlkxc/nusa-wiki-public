Bakedgoods was the twenty-fifth and forty-fifth president of the United States. His first presidency followed the removal of Procursive in early April 2019 and ended with his resignation on 21 July. His later administration in 2022 revoked earlier executive orders, reorganized internal oversight and established a Federal Combatant Coordinator.[2]

Vice presidency

On 19 February 2019, Bakedgoods was confirmed as Vice President. He thanked President Dralian for the nomination and Congress for its support.[3]

Following Procursive's presidential oath on 21 March, Bakedgoods was again nominated and sworn in as Vice President. This appointment placed him in the succession position when Procursive's presidency ended.[4]

Presidency and departure

A White House memorandum dated April 6, 2019, is signed by Bakedgoods as President and directs executive-branch departments and agencies to maintain an employee database with updated rosters. The memo lists agencies and says intelligence databases would remain classified. Its stated completion deadline predates the memo, an apparent internal date error that does not affect its identification of Bakedgoods as President.[5]

Bakedgoods resigned on 21 July 2019. Vice President Smallchomama1234 was to serve until President-elect Josh took office.[6]

Bakedgoods later held the forty-fifth presidency, between hopp_fr and Jtport. His dated executive orders establish presidential service from 29 July through at least 5 August 2022; the exact beginning and end of that later term remain unspecified.[7]

Second presidency

Bakedgoods returned as the forty-fifth President after hopp_fr and before Jtport. Executive orders dated 29 July–5 August 2022 establish actions of this administration, although its exact oath and departure dates remain unspecified.[8]

Executive orders

On July 29, 2022, Executive Order 45-001 revoked all prior executive orders except those that required a department or other entity to close. The order said the revocations took effect immediately.[9]

Also dated July 29, Executive Order 45-002 directed the Bureau of Internal Affairs to shut down and returned its powers to their original departments, effective immediately.[10]

Executive Order 45-003, dated July 30, restored Executive Order 12-006 and directed that the Special Forces and SWAT groups reopen with the same staff they had before Executive Order 45-001.[11]

An August 5 order, numbered Executive Order #0004 in its text and catalogued by the board as 45-004, established a Federal Combatant Coordinator to direct federal agencies during an invasion of Washington, D.C. The order directed federal combative and law-enforcement entities to join the Secret Service Discord. During an invasion, the coordinator would have authority over federal agencies, coordinate with agency directors and military leadership, and be selected by the President with advice from the Secretary of Defense and the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The order also invoked martial law from 12:01 a.m. on Friday, August 5, until noon the following Monday, and said the order would expire at the end of the war.[12]

All four orders are catalogued as repealed. Their repeal dates and the reasons for repeal remain unspecified.[13]

Succession and the 2019 Bar controversy

Bakedgoods's first presidency followed the removal of Procursive. The Senate convicted his predecessor on four articles and declined to disqualify him from future office. Bakedgoods was exercising presidential authority by 6 April. On 7 May, the Supreme Court invalidated three of the articles but sustained the remaining one, leaving Procursive's removal intact.[14]

During this presidency, the administration also faced a constitutional dispute over the legal profession. Bakedgoods signed S. 147 on 27 April, attempting to move regulation of the Bar into the executive branch. Kirkman's class action produced a District Court injunction, and a successor measure sought to retain the arrangement while addressing concerns affecting existing practitioners.[15]

The litigation continued through the end of Bakedgoods's presidency. On 28 July, a week after his resignation, the Supreme Court struck S. 147 down in full. Its judgment held that the executive could not administer the admission and discipline of court officers. The controversy connected the administration's legislative program with a lasting institutional dispute over the Federal Bar and separation of powers.[16]
