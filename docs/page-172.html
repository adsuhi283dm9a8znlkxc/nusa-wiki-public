2022 government reconstruction (nUSA)

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

Reorganization of government and preparations for Boulder County in 2022 The 2022 government reconstruction was a broad reorganization of national institutions, legislation and public administration. Announced in January under pauljkl and a new management leadership including Libres, it sought to rebuild a government facing low participation and prepare it for a renewed national life centered on Boulder County. The program extended across constitutional discussions, congressional elections, public employment and legal practice.[1][2]

Reconstruction was a sequence of reforms rather than a single enactment. Government continued to conduct business while institutions were being reconsidered. By the autumn, temporary elections had renewed Congress, the House had adopted new standing rules, and President TacticalRock101 was consolidating agencies in preparation for Boulder's opening.[3][4][5]

The January program

On 19 January, Speaker MasonRockefeller announced a plan assembled by pauljkl to undertake extensive reconstruction. Departments and agencies would close or change, legislation would be removed from the governing body of law, and the administration would pursue a clean-slate approach. Citizens were invited to contribute proposals and criticism. The announcement described the intended direction without itself enacting every agency closure or repeal.[6]

The promise of a fresh start did not extend to every sanction. In March, Rockefeller explained that many office bars and blacklists would be reset, while exclusions for destructive attacks on official institutions and assets would remain. Reconstruction therefore combined relief from older restrictions with the retention of penalties protecting the government's operating infrastructure.[7]

Constitutional discussions

The Speaker and President pro tempore convened a joint town hall on 4 February to discuss Congress, reconstruction and constitutional rewriting. On 25 April, Rockefeller announced that Congress had adopted H. Con. Res. 1 and S. Con. Res. 1, obtained the Supreme Court's consent and entered a constitutional convention scheduled to last until 31 May. The convention placed revision of the governing framework alongside the practical rebuilding of public offices.[8][9]

Staffing a diminished government

The House sought staff for its leadership and committees in April. Rockefeller appealed to citizens to participate while activity was low, arguing that the government needed people to rebuild institutions before a larger return of public participation. This recruitment effort made administrative capacity a central part of reconstruction: laws and revised structures required officials able to operate them.[10]

In November, TacticalRock101 addressed the same problem from the executive side. Limited personnel and resources led him to furlough agencies whose responsibilities could be absorbed elsewhere. He left open their future return as needs and activity changed. The measure concentrated personnel rather than promising to maintain every existing institution at its previous size.[11][12]

Citizenship and legal practice

The Domestic Protection Act of 2022 became an important legislative product of reconstruction. Introduced by Rockefeller on 12 July and announced as signed on 13 July, it distinguished citizenship in another American nation from holding civil office there. It removed discrimination based solely on foreign citizenship while retaining office-ineligibility provisions and a congressional process for imposing or lifting bars.[13][14]

The judiciary prepared its own reforms. On 14 July, Chief Justice RummyLeroux outlined a Federal Bar Association subordinate to the Supreme Court. The plan provided for licensing, ethical standards, paid legal services and law firms in Boulder County. Legal practice was being treated as a profession with a route toward clerkships and judicial appointments, rather than simply admission to individual cases.[15]

Interim elections and parliamentary reform

The Federal Election Commission's August schedule called for the replacement of both chambers through temporary elections: sixteen representatives and ten senators. House voting was scheduled for 20–21 August and Senate voting for 27–28 August. The temporary arrangement anticipated a later election system associated with Boulder's opening.[16]

Further House reforms followed under Rockefeller. In October he proposed tighter limits on absences and proxies, emphasizing that elected representatives should ordinarily perform their own duties. On 9 November the chamber adopted a new set of standing rules covering committees, leadership responsibilities, legislative procedure, conduct, activity and possible educational programs. By this stage, reconstruction involved not only filling seats but also defining how their occupants were expected to serve.[17][18]

Boulder and the next institutional era

Boulder County opened on 26 November 2022. Its arrival brought reconstruction's preparations into a new setting for municipal government, law enforcement and professional activity. It also generated external tensions: Rockefeller introduced H.R. 25 in response to Ridgeway's restrictions on participation, proposing protections against discrimination based on membership there. The announcement introduced a bill and sought support; it did not by itself establish enactment.[19][20]

The reconstruction preceded the separate 2023 legal reset. The two belong to successive phases of institutional change and should not be collapsed into a single event: the 2022 program rebuilt offices, procedures and participation over many months, while the later reset formally voided existing legal materials.[21]
