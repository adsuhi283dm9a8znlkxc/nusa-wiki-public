Public Law Database (nUSA)

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

The Public Law Database (PLD) is a roleplay records index linked from the NARA Hub. Its Trello board groups records into congressional-period lists and includes dedicated sections for the National Archives and Records Administration, constitutional material, and notes. Its cards preserve bill texts and status descriptions; board organization is evidence of how records were catalogued, not independent proof that every listed measure became operative law. [1]

Trello index

The PLD includes older and newer congressional-period lists, including a pre-reset list and later-period lists. It also contains records bearing labels such as active, repealed, void, failed, or archived. This structure preserves legislative history and status assertions side by side; a particular measure should be checked against its own bill or law text and associated record. [2]

NARA-related records illustrate why entries need to be read individually. Public Law 39-5 describes re-establishing an archive group conditionally “if passed”; Public Law 75-1 lists the 2019 reformation measure but marks its attached version archived; Public Law 80-2 likewise marks the Archivist Act attachment archived. Their list locations and public-law numbering do not by themselves resolve passage or implementation. [3]

Earlier NARA entries

Public Law 39-5 recounts that an earlier National Archive group, which its author said had kept a record of NUSA events, had closed for inactivity. The proposed remedy was to recreate the group and appoint a chair through presidential appointment and Senate approval; the card expressly conditions those steps on the bill passing. It is therefore a record of a proposed restoration, not proof that it occurred. [4]

Public Law 56-5 describes a “current” NARA “to be revamped” and sets out a wider records role: maintaining passed laws by Congress, executive orders by presidency, Supreme Court and federal-court decisions, and lists of offices and past officeholders. Its organization section establishes the proposed Archivist role, a Deputy Archivist, and staff. The card is undated in its text, so it supplies evidence of a later organizational proposal in the PLD’s congressional-period arrangement but not a firm calendar date. [5]

The newer PLD includes the archived S. 188 and S. 244 attachments alongside these older entries. That mixture makes the database useful as a record of proposals and institutional changes, while underscoring that an indexed law number, card title, and archived text are separate evidence from a signed enactment or demonstrated practice. [6]

2023 legal reset

On 15 July 2023, Group Management declared nUSA federal laws, case law and court precedent, and congressional resolutions, motions, and awards null and void. Its announcement said the Colorado Criminal Code and real-life U.S. Code would apply in the interim. On 17 July, a second announcement released Memorandum #001, a new Constitution, and archival requirements, and directed users to the new NARA Hub and Congress boards. The PLD’s pre-reset and later period lists preserve records on both sides of this break; list inclusion alone does not establish that an older measure remained in force afterward. [7]

Later spreadsheet announcement

On 26 February 2025, an Archivist announcement introduced a public spreadsheet intended to track active, repealed, and nullified laws in a structured and accessible form. The announcement described it as replacing or supplementing the earlier Trello index. It records an intended transition and function; it does not verify that every spreadsheet entry was complete or that the Trello board ceased to be available. [8]

The PLD is linked from the NARA Hub and relates to the National Archives Reformation Act of 2019 (nUSA) and Archivist Act of 2020 (nUSA). Its records concern nUSA roleplay institutions rather than actual U.S. legislation. [9]
