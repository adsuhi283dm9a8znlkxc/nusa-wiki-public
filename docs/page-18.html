Elections in nUSA

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

Elections in nUSA were roleplay activities conducted within the Roblox-based nUSA community to select federal and municipal officeholders. Archived announcements describe presidential, House, Senate, and municipal races, with voting taking place through Roblox games or other procedures announced by election administrators. The process and rules changed over time; the available record includes both detailed Federal Election Commission (FEC) regulations and later management directives.[1]

Election administration

The FEC organized and administered elections, and its notices directed candidates and voters to registration systems, voting locations, and complaint channels. A 2020 FEC publication described candidate eligibility checks, the administration of federal elections, ballot invalidation, and election officer oversight. Specific rules were tied to the relevant publication or election notice and should not be assumed to have governed every period identically.[2]

The archived records show that the election experience was not always frictionless. During a May 2019 election, the FEC reported a filtering delay before it announced certification and delivery of results. In August 2019, an administrator said some voters could not access voting, described a temporary alternate voting route for affected users, and warned that duplicate voting would disqualify both ballots. These are contemporary notices about particular events, not evidence of the frequency or total impact of such problems.[3]

Elections and voting venues

Presidential and congressional contests are documented separately across the archive. The 2019 presidential elections (nUSA) included Winter, Summer, and Fall races. During the Summer 2019 presidential election, the FEC's result letter reported 753 ballots received and 362 disqualified, then certified SpeakerJosh as President-elect. The Fall 2019 result letter reported a distinct election and a different eligibility determination.[4]

A 2019 schedule notice set separate dates for Washington, D.C. mayoral and council elections and specified registration, voting, and result dates. Federal congressional election notices likewise announced registration and voting phases by chamber and class. These records show distinct election calendars for different offices rather than a single universal election day.[5]

Some voting occurred in Roblox experiences advertised as election venues. Announcements pointed voters to the “USA Secure Voting Access Area,” while the FEC's later MultiBallot announcement described moving candidate petitioning to the in-game U.S. Courthouse. The MultiBallot document was a design announcement; its contents specify the proposed workflow and do not alone establish its actual use in every subsequent election.[6]

Rules and reforms

A June 2024 Group Management announcement said the FEC and development team collaborated on MultiBallot, which outlined seat-specific congressional voting, incumbent labels, multiple simultaneous races, and a voter petition tool. The accompanying document also announced three official political parties and said existing and pending parties were dissolved. These points describe the system as announced in 2024.[7]

A separate March 2024 election schedule directive addressed congressional and municipal cycles. It said the then-current House and Senate schedule conflicted with constitutional class and term provisions, assigned House elections to the first weekend of alternating months and Senate elections to the second weekend, and reserved the third and fourth weekends for municipal elections as needed. It said presidential dates took precedence. The rule belongs to that directive and period and should be read alongside the relevant constitutional version and later changes.[8]

2024 FEC election records

2024 presidential elections

The Spring 2024 presidential ballot listed GeorgeGodsent and Robbiebeastpro90000 as a ticket alongside AdamStratton, StopJosh_STOP and JustinIsNotWeird, ElijahJunaid, and AugmentedReaper. The FEC’s linked result certificate names GeorgeGodsent and Robbiebeastpro90000 the winners with 330 votes, followed by AdamStratton with 227, AugmentedReaper with 10, StopJosh_STOP and JustinIsNotWeird with 8, and ElijahJunaid with 1. The certificate reports 575 post-filter votes and identifies GeorgeGodsent as President-elect for a planned March 21 inauguration.[9]

The July ballot listed Pioneer Party nominee `@blanket` with `@cabot`, Plutocratic Party nominee GeorgeGodsent, and independent `@24craft`. The FEC result certificate names Teasoups as winner with 388 votes, ahead of GeorgeGodsent (379) and `24craft` (27), and calls Teasoups the 52nd President. Because the ballot and result document use different names for the Pioneer winner, the archive does not itself supply the identity crosswalk. On July 22, the National Elections Administrator described two possible transition dates for Teasoups after an election-schedule error; the post says Management would respect the incoming administration’s preference or a court decision, so it does not establish which option took effect.[10]

The November presidential slate listed Teasoups with Rathuzen as the Pioneer ticket, TradeNato for Forward, and independent candidates. The November 10 FEC/Group Management post announced `@blanket`’s reelection; its attached result certificate names Teasoups and Rathuzen first, with 184 voters, followed by ApolloKorman and MitsuoMochizuki for Forward (82), Jtport (6), and adamsoups (3). The certificate records an inauguration scheduled for November 23. The sources use both `@blanket` and Teasoups for the Pioneer winner; preserve those source labels unless an independent record confirms their relationship.[11]


2025 FEC election records

2025 presidential contests

The March 2025 ballot paired Rathuzen with snowbleed for the Pioneer Party, aConArtist with Frosty_TheSn0wman for Forward, and FighterHath with warlockings as independents. Rathuzen and snowbleed won with 60.75% of valid votes; the FEC declared them President-elect and Vice President-elect for a term scheduled to begin 23 March.[12]

The July schedule changed during the campaign. The accessible NEA certificate names aConArtist the 56th President, but the Discord post linking it names @zapz. as President-elect. An inaugural notice identifies aConArtist and TacticalRock101 as President-elect and Vice President-elect, schedules their ceremony for 25 July, and a later post says the inauguration was starting. The inspected July records do not include an oath transcript or explicit completion notice.[13]

The November results document certified Bathamza as President-elect and the 58th President. Although the schedule said the term would start 21 November, the NEA reported Bathamza was sworn in on 22 November alongside Vice President aConArtist. The 14 November ballot notice lists Mallsoft before an administrator announced the disqualification; the post gives no reason.[14]

Later FEC archive records

Voting systems and venues

The MultiBallot system moved from testing to use in mid-2024. The nUSA owner announced test races at the in-game U.S. Courthouse on 23 June; a later notice reopened the test to try a revised backend. On 5 July, the FEC described the House Class II contest as the first election using MultiBallot, and its 8 July results notice thanked voters for using it. This confirms operational use in that contest, while the design document remains the source for features not independently described in these announcements.[15]

In September 2026, the community announced testing a new election system in DC3. The instructions invited participants to register candidates, collect signatures, and cast test votes; during the test the normal two-hour playtime threshold was reduced to six minutes. A later September notice scheduled October federal elections with registration at the DC3 courthouse, voting booths near MPD headquarters, and an in-game schedule at the courthouse. At the time of the announcement these were trial and prospective arrangements, not a completed election record.[16]

Election administration disputes

In March 2026, a management statement reported a revised tally after an initial FEC presidential certification and a filtering review. The initial notice reported 98 votes for warlockings and 90 for Bathamza; the subsequent management notice reported 117–109 for Bathamza, without a replacement FEC certificate in this archive and said the Commission would disable immediate result displays during filtering. The statements present management's findings and response; the available archive does not independently verify the alleged access to live vote data.[17]
