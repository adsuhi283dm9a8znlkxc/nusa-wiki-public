NARA presidential records

Procursive was President of the United States in 2019 and a former director of the Secret Service. His brief presidency developed into a confrontation over congressional investigations, executive privilege and the limits of impeachment. The House impeached him on four articles on 31 March, and the Senate convicted and removed him. He subsequently won the invalidation of three articles before the Supreme Court, but the surviving charge left his removal in force.[2]

Secret Service and accession

Before entering presidential politics, Procursive rose to the directorship of the Secret Service. Dralian selected him as his running mate, then declined to assume another presidential term. Procursive consequently became President-elect and took the oath on 21 March 2019. His inaugural message promised to preserve the Constitution and defend democracy.[3][4]

Procursive called himself the twenty-third President in his oath announcement, while the National Archives' presidential sequence places him twenty-fourth, immediately after Dralian. His accession date is 21 March; the conflicting numbering does not change the order of succession.[5]

Presidency

Procursive nominated Bakedgoods as Vice President, and Bakedgoods was sworn in on the day of the presidential transition. Securing the Vice President's confirmation was one of the administration's early successes, but the President faced resistance from Congress and had limited progress on his legislative agenda.[6][7]

Around 23 March, the House Foreign Affairs Committee began investigating allegations of denial-of-service attacks and exposure of private information involving people connected with the administration's intelligence community. Procursive declined to comply with a subpoena to testify, invoking executive privilege. Disputes over his appointments intensified the confrontation: critics alleged nepotism in the employment of his cousin on his protection detail and challenged the appointment of JamesMontagu to his White House staff on national-security grounds.[8]

Impeachment and resignation controversy

Main article: Impeachment of Procursive The House unanimously adopted four impeachment articles on 31 March. They alleged false arrest, withholding information from Congress, nepotism and a national-security risk arising from Montagu's employment. The false-arrest accusation concerned conduct before Procursive became President, bringing his Secret Service career into the center of the dispute.[9]

Procursive also announced a resignation effective at midnight, then withdrew it before that time. The Supreme Court denied review of the withdrawal on 4 April in Psychodynamic v. Procursive. Five justices joined a nonbinding statement explaining that a resignation could be recalled before taking effect. The episode left the Senate proceedings, rather than a straightforward voluntary departure, central to the end of his presidency.[10]

The Senate convicted him on all four articles and removed him, but did not disqualify him from future office. Bakedgoods was acting as President by 6 April. Procursive challenged the removal, alleging defects in congressional oaths, the Senate trial and the substantive grounds of impeachment.[11][12]

Supreme Court challenge

The Court heard his case on 17 April and delivered its decision on 7 May. It rejected the oath and due-process challenges but invalidated Articles 2, 3 and 4. Applying the Ichigo standard, it concluded that those articles did not allege legitimate criminal offenses; the withholding-information charge also implicated executive privilege. Article 1 survived, and the Court denied reinstatement.[13]

The result gave Procursive a partial constitutional victory while confirming the loss of the office. His case became an important episode in the history of judicial review of impeachment, exposing sharp differences among the justices over the definition of high crimes and misdemeanors and the extent of the judiciary's authority over Congress.[14]
