Reset4K, also known as Reset3634643, is an American politician who served as the thirtieth President of the United States from 21 March to 27 June 2020. Elected as the Democratic candidate with Technozo as vice president, he defeated incumbent JamesMontagu in the winter presidential election. His administration pursued changes to federal employment, law-enforcement training, military commissions, intelligence administration and executive accountability.[2][1]

His presidency combined an extensive program of executive orders with diplomatic negotiations and repeated confrontations with Congress. An initial Senate conviction was overturned by the Supreme Court in April. In June, the Federal Election Commission disqualified him from seeking reelection, and a second impeachment ended in his conviction and removal. Technozo succeeded him before BernardCaldwell won the summer election.[3][4][5]

Reset later returned to congressional leadership as Speaker of the House in August 2022. During a period of institutional reconstruction, he advocated transparency, a public process for reviewing officeholding bars and renewed participation by returning citizens.[6][7]

Presidential campaigns

Summer 2019 election

Reset contested the summer 2019 presidential election as an independent under the name Reset3634643. He finished second with 137 votes, behind Republican SpeakerJosh, who received 209. Blastatack received 32 and Kind_Yada 13. The Federal Election Commission considered a motion to disqualify Reset, but it failed by one vote in favor, three against and two abstentions. His candidacy therefore remained eligible through certification of the result.[8]

Winter 2020 election

In the winter 2020 election, Reset ran as the Democratic candidate against JamesMontagu and a wider field. The certified result gave Reset 277 votes and Montagu 242, a margin of 35. The Commission reported receiving 1,078 ballots and excluding 493 for alternate accounts or failure to hold citizenship. It declared Reset president-elect on 17 February and certified Technozo as his running mate after the required notice to Congress.[2]

The election returned a new president while preserving a scheduled interval between certification and accession. Reset took the oath on 21 March, succeeding Montagu. His administration began with a series of cabinet appointments and measures intended to organize federal agencies and the transfer of executive responsibilities.[1][9]

Presidency

Inauguration and administration

Reset appointed AlexanderFitzpatrick White House chief of staff on 21 March. KaceyMontagu and largeTitanic2 were nominated and confirmed that day as secretaries of State and the Treasury respectively. TacticalRock101 was confirmed as secretary of Defense on 28 March. In April, iBuzBae became secretary of State and ThePelicanator secretary of Commerce and Labor, both following nominations on 14 April and Senate confirmations on 16 April.[11]

Judicial appointments included AlexJCabot as chief district judge. Reset nominated him on 14 April, and the Senate confirmed him two days later by 16 votes to one. Reset also nominated Tony_Giordano to the Supreme Court on 17 June; the Senate confirmed him unanimously.[12]

Transition planning and executive coordination

Reset established a White House Transition Coordinating Council to organize succession planning, prepare briefing material and improve communication between agencies and incoming presidential teams. The chief of staff chaired the council, which included senior advisers and representatives of eligible candidates. A Federal Transition Coordinator was to assemble information needed by the president-elect and serve through the first month of the incoming administration. Selection and dismissal of the coordinator rested with the incoming president, while the outgoing president was prohibited from terminating the appointment.[13]

A separate order created a Homeland Security Council to advise the president on domestic threats and coordinate senior law-enforcement officials. It placed the presidential homeland-security adviser in the chair, with the secretary and deputy secretary of Homeland Security as co-chairs. Its responsibilities included regular meetings, reports to the president and communication during attacks or other security emergencies. Federal agencies were represented alongside municipal and military officials, bringing several jurisdictions into a common advisory structure.[14]

Other orders addressed executive conduct and public communication. His religious-liberty order directed agencies to protect religious and political expression while retaining restrictions on harmful, derogatory or explicit conduct. An announcement policy allowed the president, vice president and cabinet to reserve an important national group announcement for twelve hours during a crisis. Judicial officers, congressional leadership and Management were exempt from that executive restriction.[15]

Intelligence and employee vetting

Employee vetting became a substantial part of the administration's security policy. An initial order granted designated agency leaders access to ECHELON and created Special Source Operations within the National Security Agency to examine prospective federal employees. A revision transferred those functions to an Internal Vetting Operations Division within Homeland Security. Employers were to report existing staff for review, while new employees required clearance before beginning their duties. Ordinary applications were to be cleared or flagged within twenty-four hours, with an exception for intelligence appointments and provision for different standards according to the position.[16]

On 16 April, Reset established a six-member presidential commission to oversee the transfer of assets and information from ECHELON. The commission could organize replacement recording systems, administer clearances for the transfer and issue directives to executive departments and agency heads. The president retained authority to appoint and remove its members. The order made information management and classified-material transfers part of a wider reorganization of the federal government.[17]

Law-enforcement training and employment

On 21 April, Reset established a temporary Federal Law Enforcement Training Center under Homeland Security. It was to certify federal law-enforcement personnel and publish successful certifications. The president appointed its director and the chair of a School for National and Homeland Security Affairs, which would develop arrangements for exempted agencies. The training center could deny certification but could not directly dismiss personnel from their employing agencies; officers who failed certification faced suspension, and employers were to terminate those who failed twice.[18]

The order initially remained in force until 11 May, subject to presidential renewal. Reset presented it as a trial whose results would inform a permanent institution developed with the Plutocratic caucus. Its examination requirements depended on a further curriculum order, distinguishing the establishment of the training authority from the commencement of every certification requirement.[18][19]

A companion employment initiative directed the secretary of Commerce and Labor to develop a United States Employment Center connecting prospective employees with vacancies. Reset intended it eventually to serve as an employment hub for certified law-enforcement personnel. The policy linked wider access to government work with his proposed training system, rather than treating recruitment and certification as separate concerns.[20]

The administration also introduced a White House internship program on 7 May. It provided for twenty interns, with ten to be selected for staff positions and the remainder eligible for further advancement. Duties included tracking presidential documents, assisting departments and arranging authorized tours. Applications were to open every four weeks. Security restrictions limited access to gates and the presidential emergency facilities, and interns had to sign a nondisclosure agreement.[21]

Military organization

Reset's military reforms included an order of 19 April standardizing Officer Training School and Officer Candidate School. Programs were to comprise aptitude testing and interviews, instruction and examination in military doctrine, and final processing with published results. An Officer Promotion Review Board chaired by the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff would approve or reject commissions by majority vote. Service secretaries were restricted to direct commissioning of warrant officers, while branch training commands retained responsibility for assessing candidates.[22]

On 13 May, he placed the Coast Guard under the Department of the Navy in both wartime and peacetime. The order cited inactivity and insufficient oversight, assigned administrative and operational supervision to the secretary of the Navy, and provided for Defense oversight when required. It retained the Coast Guard's military ranks, made its commandant and vice commandant members of the Joint Chiefs, and applied the officer-training requirements introduced in April. Maritime protection, rescue and support for wartime operations formed its stated duties.[23]

Clemency, merit and disciplinary powers

On 25 April, Reset created a Clemency and Pardon Advisory Board to consider wrongful convictions and disproportionate sentences. A presidentially appointed chair and co-chair would organize review, with seven delegates considering applications. The board could investigate, hear voluntary testimony and recommend relief, while the final decision to issue a pardon remained with the president. Its procedures included majority voting and opportunities for attorneys to submit applications.[24]

His Merit for All order of 30 April created a Merit Systems Protection Board to consider complaints about unfair promotion decisions, abuse and mistreatment of federal employees. The board fell under the training center and consisted of a chair, deputy chair and three members. Its role was advisory: it could recommend action to the president but could not directly intervene in an agency. The president retained appointment and dismissal authority over the board.[25]

The administration also retained executive employment blacklists. An order dated 29 April defined four tiers: three weeks, four weeks, eight weeks and permanent exclusion. Designated senior officials could impose them, employers were required to check the registers before hiring, and an appeal process was to be available through the Executive Office of the President.[26]

Reset separately established the Presidential Medal of Extraordinary Service for exceptional heroism or service, including prevention of foreign or domestic disasters. The order required evidence or supporting witnesses and limited awards to two per presidential term. It prohibited sale or forgery of the distinction and required publication of a transcript or recording when an award ceremony was private.[27]

Relations with Congress and the Plutocrats

Reset initially worked with the Plutocratic Party on parts of his legislative and administrative agenda. On 19 April, he announced a meeting with the party's Senate caucus and promised cooperation to reduce military bureaucracy. His temporary training-center proposal likewise envisaged a permanent institution developed with the caucus. SurpriseParty returned to Senate leadership during this period, and a Congressional Petitions Center announced on 30 April was presented by GeorgeGodsent as a joint initiative with her as Senate majority leader.[28][19]

By late June, public discussion reflected a rupture with the older party leadership. On 25 June, BernardCaldwell predicted that SurpriseParty and SheldonParty would take the presidency from Reset, while other participants described opposition within the Plutocratic Senate. The earlier cooperation had therefore given way to a public confrontation during the reelection campaign and second impeachment. These assessments concerned the political alignment of the dispute; the legal actions against Reset proceeded through the Commission, House and Senate.[29]

Foreign policy

Diplomatic initiatives

Reset pursued diplomatic contacts soon after taking office. On 23 March, he met Finland's president and prime minister to discuss a treaty and United Nations matters. The next day he met Bahrain's king and delegates, with Finland's prime minister also attending. These meetings accompanied an agenda of treaty negotiations and cooperation with foreign governments.[30]

War with the United Kingdom

The April 2020 war with the United Kingdom began during Reset's presidency. The United States Military's later account dates the opening of fighting to 2 April. On 11 April, Reset announced that he had spoken with the king and expected a treaty recognizing a stalemate. The military subsequently identified the settlement as the Hillsborough Accords and credited it with securing American sovereignty.[31][32]

Russia, Ukraine and foreign security

On 21 April, Reset met Russia's prime minister and placed the United States at REDCON2, describing intervention in the Russia–Ukraine dispute as likely. The following day, he briefed the military on attacks he attributed to the Pakistani government and announced American withdrawal from the Russia–Ukraine conflict. In June, he met the Russian president to discuss NATO–CSTO relations and other diplomatic issues.[33]

His foreign-security order of 25 April cited attacks attributed to Pakistani representatives as grounds for stronger domestic safeguards. It regulated the detention of designated foreign threats, required coordination between Homeland Security and Justice, and directed publication of national-security designations. It also restricted unauthorized dealings with foreign representatives, protected invited foreign guests against threats and required executive employees to leave designated hostile nations within seventy-two hours. Ordinary foreign membership was not itself grounds for dismissal under the order.[34]

Impeachments and removal

April impeachment and reinstatement

An impeachment trial was scheduled for 4 April, with restricted entry to the Senate proceedings and a public broadcast. Following a Senate conviction, the Supreme Court issued an administrative stay that allowed Reset to remain president while his challenge was considered.[35]

The first impeachment concerned alleged witness tampering, obstruction of a congressional committee and conspiracy to impede a law-enforcement officer. On 20 April, the Supreme Court overturned the removal in Reset4K v. United States. It rejected his procedural and political-motive challenges but held that the surviving articles did not allege constitutionally impeachable offenses. The opinion was released on 26 April.[3]

Reelection campaign and disqualification

Reset sought reelection in the summer contest. A Fox News debate and town hall announced for 22 May included him alongside NikolaKalinic, RandomBlox and BernardCaldwell. The campaign subsequently became the subject of an official complaint.[36][4]

On 25 June, the Federal Election Commission disqualified Reset from the presidential election. Its letter found a violation of Article XII of the Federal Election Regulations and related summer campaign rules. Those provisions governed expenditures intended to influence voting and political advocacy exchanged for money, employment, appointment, contracts or other benefits. The ruling allowed an appeal through the Commission.[4][37]

June impeachment and succession

The House approved three articles of impeachment on 25 June, separately from the election disqualification. The articles alleged offers of money to Representative Krenth for an official apology and a Senate seat to Representative Jtdalejr88 for his resignation; campaign funds offered to a potential voter for a Jailbreak game pass; and military authority used to pressure servicemembers to support him. These were the House's allegations; the subsequent conviction notice specified Article I.[5][3]

On 27 June, the Supreme Court denied a preemptive stay, finding that Reset had not demonstrated likely success on the merits. The ruling concerned interim relief rather than a final judgment on all three charges.[3]

The Senate convicted Reset on Article I that day, ending his presidency. Technozo took the presidential oath immediately afterward. The succession therefore followed the vice president's accession, rather than a direct transfer to BernardCaldwell, who won the subsequent summer election and took office in July.[5][39]

Later career

Speaker of the House, 2022

Reset returned to congressional leadership in August 2022, succeeding MasonRockefeller as Speaker. Jtport welcomed him on 22 August, and Reset delivered his own opening address that evening. His speakership formed part of efforts to restore federal participation during the period of reconstruction following Ridgeway's separation.[6][7]

In his address, Reset blamed corruption, hostility and the Ridgeway rupture for damage to national institutions, while calling for impartiality and transparency. He proposed an “unbar hub” through which returning citizens could seek a second opportunity to hold office. A further announcement that evening offered a public review process involving meetings with congressional members by voice or text. His attribution of the earlier decline to Ridgeway was his political assessment, while the proposed review mechanism sought to restore participation and make congressional leadership answerable to applicants.[7]

On 22 August, he formally invited the president to deliver a State of the Union address to a joint session of Congress on 28 August. The invitation described instability and rapid changes of leadership and asked the administration to explain its plans for national revitalization. On 25 August, Reset published new standing rules for the House. His office also advertised recruitment for a Capitol Police chief and a channel for direct contact with the Speaker.[40]

His speakership ended in September. The congressional succession places the end of his tenure on 12 September and the beginning of GameLab2017's on 14 September. The circumstances of his departure are uncertain.[6]

Succession to GameLab2017

His successor GameLab2017 was elected on 14 September 2022, when an official announcement scheduled the new Speaker’s oath for that evening.[41]

Presidential records

Reset's presidential papers are administered by the National Archives and Records Administration through the Reset4K Presidential Traphouse. His executive-order series runs from 30-001 to 30-020 and includes a revision of the employee-vetting order. The surviving policies encompass transition arrangements, security coordination, federal recruitment, military training, employment protections and clemency.[42]
