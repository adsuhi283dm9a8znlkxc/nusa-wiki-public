Senate (nUSA)

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

Upper chamber in the federal legislature The Senate is the upper chamber of Congress, alongside the House. It considers legislation, nominations and inter-chamber business and conducts impeachment trials. Leadership, committees and standing rules organize its proceedings.[1]

Chamber records and procedure

The Senate has organized membership into electoral classes, including Class I and Class II, and maintained leadership offices including the Senate President and secretary. Standing rules govern proceedings, while agendas and prescribed forms organize bills and simple, concurrent and joint resolutions.[2]

Senate seats have been filled through distinct electoral classes, with certification following voting. In May 2019 the Federal Elections Commission certified seven Class III winners. By 21 July the Senate was led by ClutchAntonioVII; its business included quorum rules and discussion of a proposed NARA Reformation Act.[3]

Senate's role in inter-chamber proceedings

The Senate receives articles of impeachment passed by the House. In July 2018, the House forwarded BernardCaldwell's impeachment for Senate consideration. In October, the Senate took the separate step of lifting his office bar. Referral for trial and later restoration of eligibility were different actions.[4]

The July 2023 reset voided existing federal laws, case precedent and congressional resolutions and motions. A new Constitution and legislative directives followed, permitting normal congressional business to resume under a revised framework.[5]

Archives and interpretation

The Congress index provides Senate-floor, nomination, resolution, treaty, impeachment and constitutional-amendment lists, while the Senate-specific board preserves chamber procedures and office records. A draft, floor item, or proposal must therefore be distinguished from an adopted measure; the Congress board separately lists failed items and passed or active records.[6]

Impeachment and reconstruction

The Senate's trial of Procursive in 2019 culminated in conviction on all four impeachment articles and removal from the presidency, but not disqualification from future office. The Supreme Court subsequently invalidated three articles and left the remaining charge intact. That outcome exposed the distinction between the Senate's verdict and the constitutional validity of each charge supporting it.[7]

Impeachment did not invariably end in removal. On 22 October 2022, the Senate acquitted Jtport on every charge brought before it. The proceeding occurred during a period of disputes over the chamber's own administration. In September, Secretary of the Senate hankwim55 resigned and accused the President pro tempore of exceeding his authority over proxies, committees and staffing. Those were the resigning secretary's allegations, distinct from a judicial finding. He remained a senator through Jtport's trial and announced his departure from the Senate on 2 November.[8][9]

Reconstruction also changed the Senate's electoral arrangements. The August 2022 interim schedule called for ten senators and the replacement of the entire chamber, with voting on 27–28 August. TacticalRummy administered oaths to newly elected senators on 28 August before the State of the Union gathering. The temporary arrangement was part of preparations for Boulder County and anticipated later elections under a new system.[10][11]
