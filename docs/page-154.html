TimGeithner (also styled Timothy Geithner) was the 19th President of the Roblox nUSA roleplay community. He took office on 30 January 2018 after President SheldonParty resigned and served until the July presidential transition to BernardCaldwell. A former Vice President, senator, justice, defense secretary, and political writer, Geithner is also associated with a constitution adopted in September 2017 and later restored by Group Management. His presidency concentrated on preserving government records, reorganizing intelligence and national-security institutions, and defining employment and veterans' policies.[2]

Earlier career and political activity

Geithner held multiple federal roles before becoming President. His contemporary author biography identified service as Vice President, Supreme Court justice, secretary of defense, and senator. During SuddenRush12G's 2016 reelection campaign, Geithner led campaign planning, drafted the 8 June announcement speech, and later became the ticket's vice-presidential candidate. He subsequently chaired the campaign of TheySinned. A December 2016 account of SuddenRush12G's administration also lists Geithner as a former White House press secretary and deputy secretary of state.[3]

He was active in diplomacy and party debate. In 2016, Geithner argued that nUSA should remain in the roleplay United Nations after a conflict with the United Kingdom strained its standing there. He had earlier served briefly as deputy secretary of state during a dispute over international recognition. He later urged the Democratic Party to organize around clear policy goals. In November 2017, he endorsed LargeTitanic2 over AcidRaps in that winter's presidential contest.[4]

Geithner authored a constitution adopted on 8 September 2017. It was readopted in January 2024 and May 2025, making his constitutional work influential across several later governments.[5]

Accession and archival policy

Geithner succeeded SheldonParty when the President and several colleagues resigned on 30 January 2018. The next day, he established a Federal Register for executive orders and scheduled earlier orders for repeal on 20 February. Selected measures were to be rewritten into a single new instrument. The surviving record does not confirm that every repeal and republication occurred on schedule.[6]

His second order required cabinet members, agency directors, senior military officers, and other designated officials to preserve official documents and transfer their records through the White House chief of staff when leaving office. Near the end of his term, a separate order directed executive departments and agencies to lock and archive their Trello boards and Twitter accounts at noon on 21 July 2018 for transfer to NARA. Together, these measures made the production and preservation of records a recurring responsibility of the executive branch.[7]

National security and intelligence

Geithner's February national-security order specified the membership and procedure of the National Security Council. It made the President its chair, placed the Vice President and the state and defense secretaries among required attendees, identified military and intelligence advisers, and called for meetings at least every two weeks. A Principals Committee was designed to coordinate senior officials on national-security matters.[8]

On 14 February, he reorganized intelligence under a director of national intelligence. The order placed the National Security Agency and Central Intelligence Agency in a central Intelligence Community, left the Federal Bureau of Investigation under the attorney general with shared intelligence oversight, dissolved several other intelligence offices, and set classification and clearance rules. A broader order signed on 20 May revised that framework: it placed the National Security Agency under Defense, made the Central Intelligence Agency report directly to the President, and expanded the National Security Council's role in reviewing covert operations. These successive orders show a change in the administration's institutional design within one term; they should not be treated as simultaneous descriptions of a single unchanged hierarchy.[9]

The administration also created a National Background Investigations Bureau under the director of national intelligence. It was assigned background checks for federal hires, with a new screening system to be prepared by the intelligence director. A 6 May order dissolved the U.S. Special Forces, ended its ongoing operations, and offered personnel a transfer to another uniformed service. A complete record of those transfers has not been located.[10]

Employment and veterans' affairs

Geithner established an Office of Government Ethics to investigate adverse-employment complaints against executive supervisors. The office could recommend dismissal, suspension, or termination, with the President making a final decision subject to district-court appeal. In March, he limited most executive employees to two departments or agencies, while generally restricting presidential appointees and civil officers to a single post. He also sought a chair for the National Labor Dispute Board, an additional employment-review body available under law.[11]

His 27 March veterans' affairs order dissolved the stand-alone Department of Veterans' Affairs and replaced it with a Veterans' Affairs Administration under the Department of Defense. It assigned the new administration job training and reemployment functions. An April order then granted registered veterans specified employment accommodations: permission to work in three executive bodies rather than the usual two, waivers from combat-related academy phases, and seven days of absence per month. The two instruments combined a departmental reorganization with individual service benefits.[12]

Final days and legacy

On 21 July 2018, Geithner directed the defense and state secretaries, attorney general, and national security adviser to review the possibility of a roleplay Space Force and report options and draft legislation by 1 August. The order initiated a study rather than establishing the service. BernardCaldwell succeeded Geithner the same day; the exact hour of the transfer has not been established.[13]

Geithner's constitutional work remained prominent well after his presidency. In May 2025, Group Management restored his constitution and revived court precedent formed under it. Management also proposed a convention to update provisions it regarded as outdated. The intervening years had brought other constitutional arrangements, so the readoption marked a distinct change in the legal framework.[14]
