NUSA archival policy

  • Page
  • Discussion
Appearance
From nUSA Wiki

The NUSA archival policy refers to rules and statements recorded for handling government and community records in the nUSA roleplay project. The sources include a broad NARA Hub statement permitting deletion, redaction, or censorship to protect people working abroad and to prevent conflict, as well as more specific 2023 requirements for public records and legislative submissions. These are community governance records and do not describe real United States archival law. [1]

Access and redaction

The Hub carries a statement that documents “may be deleted, redacted, or censored in any fashion” for the stated purposes of protecting the identities of people serving abroad and protecting the United States from conflict. The same wording appears on the Public Law Database board. The statement records broad discretion on those boards; by itself it does not identify a particular deletion decision or establish how frequently the policy was used. [2]

Preservation and review

The July 2023 Constitution assigns NARA responsibility for storing and maintaining public records for Congress, the judiciary, and the executive, and for setting retention schedules and archival processes. Group Management Memorandum #001 describes NARA as overseen by Group Management while operating autonomously from the player government. Read together, the instruments place recordkeeping within the community’s administrative framework while describing operational independence from elected roleplay government. [3]

The memorandum also sets requirements for legislative submissions, including standardized templates and metadata. It says NARA may decline archival approval for noncompliant passed bills and that such a bill does not become active law; the archival check is limited to compliance rather than policy substance. This is a specific workflow described in the 2023 memorandum, separate from the broader redaction statement on the Trello boards. [4]

Applied requirements and continuity

An August 2024 notice applied record requirements to congressional session labels. It warned that bills carrying the wrong session number would no longer be archived or logged, while granting an exception to bills already moving through the legislative process. The notice named the 98th Congress as beginning on 19 August. It presents a concrete rule about how records were labeled and logged, including a transition exception, rather than a general decision about legislation’s substance. [5]

A June 2024 quarterly-audit announcement described a separate continuity measure. NARA said it restored D.C. City Council records removed because of the holder’s personal security concerns, and that it had backed up boards to separate storage points, at frequent but undisclosed times. The notice links availability and restoration to community safety and says the backups were meant to reduce the risk posed by an “Admin Attack.” This is an announced practice at a particular date, not proof of a permanent schedule or a complete backup history. [6]

These examples show that archival practice combined record formatting, access decisions, restoration, and backup. The source record supports describing the announced rules and individual reported actions; it does not justify assuming that every board was complete, that every rule was applied uniformly, or that every deletion or acceptance decision followed the same procedure. [7]
