Lumidas was the fourth, sixth, and tenth president of the Roblox nUSA roleplay community. His administration records range from a response to civil unrest and a proposed career-guidance program to changes in agency recruitment and military command. A 2017 sequence of presidents lists three separate Lumidas presidencies; he served in 2013–2014, again in 2014, and in 2015.[1][2]

Presidential terms

The 2017 list places Lumidas after Marinio, then after Zeyad567, and later after Jcmand, with Zeyad567 following his tenth presidency. NARA’s later roster identifies these entries as the fourth, sixth, and tenth presidencies. His three periods in office ran from 30 December 2013 to 25 February 2014, 30 July to 23 September 2014, and 15 February to 20 July 2015.[1][3]

Fourth presidency

An order archived as Executive Order #4-001 directed federal agencies to deploy to Washington, D.C., after what it described as rioting, protests, and the start of a war with the United Kingdom. It raised the alert to DEFCON 2 and called for continuous federal staffing and intelligence monitoring. The archival copy lists the original date as unknown, so the events and response cannot be placed more precisely in Lumidas’s fourth term from this document alone.[4]

Sixth presidency

An order establishing a Commission of Academies proposed centralized supervision of recruitment academies at federal agencies. It assigned the commission to the Department of Education and directed affected agencies to set up academies, with deadlines in August 2014. The document is catalogued as Executive Order #4-002, but its text calls itself Lumidas’s “first executive order as 6th President.” For that reason, its contents cannot be assigned confidently to the fourth or sixth term from the archive numbering alone.[5]

Tenth presidency

The surviving #10 series gives the clearest account of Lumidas’s military policy. Executive Order #10-001 established a War Council composed of the President, interim commander in chief, and the secretaries of state and defense, with senior military and intelligence officials participating as advisers or at the President’s invitation. The council was authorized to advise on military matters and deploy forces during crises or war. The order also prescribed detention, dismissal, and treason proceedings for members who leaked intelligence.[6]

Three further orders revised military recruitment and command. Lumidas abolished obstacle-course tryouts at federal agencies, citing their limited relevance to work in the country’s cities and favoring combat simulations, reaction tests, and grammar tests. He authorized the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff to decommission military officers. A fourth order created a Military Enlistment Overseer to review enlistment policy, applications, and related discipline. The archival covers give no original signing dates for these orders, so they document the policies attributed to the tenth presidency without establishing their precise order of implementation or duration.[7]

Orders catalogued in the #4 series

Other orders catalogued in the #4 series addressed campaign activity on official USA properties, required senior officials to answer USA-related messages, and proposed a “What Job Should I Get?” career-guidance program. The program was described as a planned group and place where staff would guide citizens toward political, law-enforcement, or business careers; the order anticipated a 29 September 2014 launch. The available document describes the plan and launch date but does not confirm that the service opened. Because Executive Order #4-002 identifies itself as Lumidas’s first order as sixth President, the catalog numbers alone do not establish which term each #4-series order belongs to.[8]

Other policy records

A separate Lumidas proposal submitted as Public Law 28-1 would have opened USA audit logs to middle- and upper-class citizens. In his message to Congress, Lumidas framed the measure as a way for citizens to identify corruption and learn from the group’s history, while limiting prosecution to activity recorded after the bill became law. The surviving archive includes his request and signature, but does not include a congressional vote or a separate enactment record. The document does not establish which of his presidential terms it belongs to.[9]

Career and legacy

Lumidas later served as director of the National Security Agency under President SuddenRush12G, according to a December 2016 profile of that administration. In a November 2016 essay, former Vice President TimGeithner recalled working with Lumidas’s diplomatic team during negotiations over international recognition in 2015. Geithner credited the team with winning recognition for nUSA over a rival United States roleplay group, while noting his own short service as a deputy secretary of state during the negotiations. The essay is a participant’s retrospective account, rather than a contemporaneous diplomatic record.[10]
