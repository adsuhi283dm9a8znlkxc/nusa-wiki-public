NARA Presidential Records roster

Antonio T. Roosevelt (account name AntonioTRoosevelt) was identified by the nUSA roleplay community's archives as its 28th President. The records reviewed here concern the simulated government of the Roblox group; they do not describe the real United States government. The Presidential Records board places Roosevelt between JoshMiller, listed as the 27th President, and JamesMontagu, listed as the 29th. A separate presidential-library card also calls him the 28th President.[2]

Presidency and election

The Federal Election Commission's plan for the Fall 2019 election described the next presidential term as running from 21 November 2019 through 21 March 2020. Its ballot, published in nUSA's #announcements channel on 17 October, included Roosevelt as the Republican candidate. The contemporaneous election letter reported 653 votes, 30 disqualified as alternate accounts, and 220 votes for Roosevelt, with five of those votes disqualified. JamesMontagu received 322 votes, with 11 disqualified.[3]

The result letter named JamesMontagu the winner and President-elect but also said an injunction meant he did not yet qualify for office. Applying the cited 20th Amendment provision, the National Elections Administrator declared vice-President-elect TomFoley acting President-elect until a President qualified. The announcement record changed later that day: in #announcements, Aerium first congratulated TomFoley after posting the letter, then wrote, “Tom not gon' be President, Jame gon' be President.” This sequence records the initial ruling and its rapid change in public presentation; it does not explain the legal or procedural basis for the change.[4]

Roosevelt was in office during this election period: an Executive Archives card says he nominated AdremiCross as Secretary of Veterans Affairs on 8 October 2019, and that the Senate confirmed the nomination on 13 October. The same card records AdremiCross's resignation on 5 November. On 20 November, the #nusa-updates channel announced that President-elect Montagu would be inaugurated the following night. A message the next day linked to JamesMontagu's presidential inauguration, and Roosevelt posted that it had been a “really good 3 months” and signed off. The post does not by itself identify his role, but the roster sequence and Montagu's inauguration place the end of Roosevelt's presidency on 21 November 2019; he entered office on 29 August 2019.[1][5]

The archival numbering was itself corrected. The Executive Archives created Roosevelt's former-President card on 25 October 2019 as “AntonioTRoosevelt, 27th,” then changed its title to “AntonioTRoosevelt, 28th” on 18 November. The Presidential Records roster and the library card likewise give him the 28th number. The earlier card title is a reason to treat the ordinal as the archive's eventual classification rather than assume every contemporary record used it consistently.[6]

Prior office and administration

Before the presidency, the Executive Archives listed Roosevelt as a former Vice President. Its card, created on 16 September 2019, identified him as the 34th Vice President. A later Presidential Records card identifies him as the 37th Vice President; its February 2024 activity log shows that the card's ordinal was changed from 35th to 37th. The records do not explain the numbering changes or give dates for the vice-presidential service represented by the later card, so the two entries cannot be combined into a settled chronology.[7]

Two orders are catalogued under the 28th-presidency sequence. Executive Order 28-001 is titled “Creation of the Office of Government Ethics,” and Executive Order 28-002 “Dissolution of the Special Forces.” The Presidential Records board currently marks both cards “Repealed.” The board entries link to separate documents, but the local source archive contains only the card metadata, not the order texts; their provisions, dates, implementation, and reasons for repeal are therefore not established here.[8]

The legislative archive also indexes Senate Concurrent Resolution 22, “To encourage President Roosevelt to resign,” with “Passed Senate” and “Passed House” labels. Senate Resolution 190, “The Roosevelt Treaty,” is labeled “Treaty” and “Passed Senate.” These are the titles and status labels preserved on the board cards; the available entries do not establish the resolutions' terms, legal effect, or whether the treaty was ultimately ratified.[9]

Archival record and later listings

The Presidential Records board's library card says Roosevelt's “complete works” were transferred to a library managed by NARA's Presidential Archives division and library staff, and links to a Trello page named rooseveltlibrary. This is the archive's own description of the collection; the card alone does not verify that the collection is complete or that all listed records remain accessible. The collection is part of the broader nUSA presidential-library system.[10]

The available sources document Roosevelt as a presidential candidate, officeholder, former Vice President, and subject of later legislative records, but do not provide a reliable full account of his later public roles. For the election chronology and the archives responsible for preserving these records, see 2019 presidential elections, United States Congress, and NARA.[11]
